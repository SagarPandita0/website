The Nearest Neighbor Attack: Fancy Bear’s Wi-Fi Hijinks and What We Can Learn

Sagar Pandita · December 23, 2024 · 4 min read

Let’s set the scene: You’re cozied up in your home office, casually scrolling through the latest security research, when you stumble upon something so audacious it makes your coffee go cold.

It’s a story about how Fancy Bear — yes, that Fancy Bear, a.k.a. APT28, a.k.a. GruesomeLarch — hacked into an enterprise Wi-Fi network not directly, but by hopping through a neighbor’s Wi-Fi like some kind of cyber ninja.

Curious? So was I. This fascinating caper, uncovered by Volexity, reads like a spy novel for the tech world. Let me walk you through it.

The Setup: Wi-Fi Hopping with Style

The attack starts like a classic thriller: bad guys want in. The target? A company with projects linked to Ukraine. But this wasn’t your typical phishing scam or brute force break-in.

Instead, Fancy Bear decided to do a little neighborly borrowing. Using a clever technique now dubbed the Nearest Neighbor Attack, they bypassed MFA restrictions and jumped networks in a way that’s as brilliant as it is terrifying.

Here’s what they did:

  1. Snagged Some Credentials: First, they ran password-spray attacks on the target company (let’s call them Organization A) to collect usernames and passwords. While MFA stopped them from accessing most systems, the enterprise Wi-Fi didn’t have MFA. Bingo.
  2. Pivoted Through a Neighbor: When they couldn’t connect directly to Organization A’s Wi-Fi, they compromised another company (Organization B) next door. There, they found a “dual-homed” system — one with wired and wireless connections.
  3. The Final Leap: Using Organization B’s dual-homed setup, they authenticated to Organization A’s Wi-Fi network. They didn’t just find a loophole — they exploited the whole neighborhood.

And it didn’t stop there. They leveraged a third company’s network (Organization C) to complete the chain, proving that proximity is indeed a vulnerability.

What Makes This Attack So Genius?

Fancy Bear didn’t just exploit technical weaknesses; they turned physical proximity into a weapon.

Imagine this: Your office Wi-Fi requires only a username and password. You’re confident that no one outside the building can connect. But what if someone across the street has a compromised device that bridges into your network? That’s exactly what happened here.

And the attackers were good at covering their tracks:

  • They used Cipher.exe, a legitimate Windows tool, to securely delete files.
  • They dumped Active Directory databases via shadow copies and staged data for exfiltration in public directories, blending into normal traffic.
  • When investigators got close, they wiped systems clean, leaving little evidence behind.

It’s like a digital game of hide-and-seek, and Fancy Bear had years of practice.

Cracking the Case

What’s incredible about Volexity’s research is how they pieced everything together. Initially, investigators assumed the attackers might be lurking in a car or building nearby. But Wi-Fi logs told a different story.

By analyzing MAC addresses and NetBIOS queries, they traced the attack to neighboring organizations. Organization B’s dual-homed device acted as a bridge, connecting the attacker to Organization A’s Wi-Fi.

It was like watching a cyber spy thriller unfold in real-time.

Lessons for All of Us

While this attack targeted specific organizations, the lessons are universal. Here’s what we can learn:

  1. Enable MFA Everywhere: Wi-Fi included. Yes, even your Wi-Fi deserves multi-factor authentication love.
  2. Check for Dual-Homed Devices: These systems are convenient, but they can also be a gateway for attackers.
  3. Log, Log, Log: Without detailed wireless controller logs, tracking this attack would’ve been nearly impossible.
  4. Think Beyond Your Walls: Your security isn’t just about your network — it’s about your neighbors, too.

Final Thoughts

The Nearest Neighbor Attack is a masterclass in creativity, showing how determined attackers can exploit the tiniest weaknesses. It’s a reminder to think holistically about security.

So next time you set up a Wi-Fi network, take a moment to imagine what Fancy Bear might do. And then, do everything you can to make their lives miserable.

Stay safe out there!
